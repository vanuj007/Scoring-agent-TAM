# Prime Care: Brand Voice Guide

_Discovered from getprime.care on 7 Oct 2026. Sources: home, /about, /coverage, /blog, 7 blog posts, terms, privacy, plus the advisor WhatsApp template sheet (8 call-outcome variations)._

---

## 1. Brand at a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Prime Care (operated by Zap Private Limited, benefits powered by Alyve Health) |
| **What it is** | An annual OPD (outpatient) benefit plan: doctor visits, diagnostics, pharmacy, dental, vision, paid cashless via an OPD wallet and Alyve QR |
| **Positioning line** | "India's Honest Health Plan" |
| **Core hook** | "Your health insurance covers hospital bills. Prime covers the rest." |
| **Mission** | "To delight millions of Indians with better health through accessible, affordable primary care - built for everyday life." |
| **Audience** | Urban, digitally native Indians aged 25–40 with a gap in OPD cover: salaried employees whose group cover excludes OPD, freelancers with no employer cover, and young families with recurring everyday health spend |
| **Enemy** | Not other insurers. The real opponent is the **gap**: everyday out-of-pocket health spend that traditional insurance ignores, plus the jargon, fine print and agent-led selling around it |

## 2. Personality

Prime Care sounds like **a well-informed friend who has read the fine print for you.** It is practical, warm and direct, and a little impatient with how complicated health insurance has been made.

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| **Honest:** we disclose every rupee upfront | Salesy, hype-driven, "best-in-class" |
| **Everyday:** we talk about fevers, blood tests, dental fillings | Catastrophic, fear-led, ICU-and-ambulance imagery |
| **Simple:** short sentences, plain words, a number wherever we can give one | Jargon-heavy, legalistic, "sum insured subject to sub-limits as per schedule" |
| **Warm and on your side:** "it's not her fault for assuming otherwise" | Preachy or blaming ("you should have read your policy") |
| **Confident and quick:** "Buy today, use tomorrow." | Breathless or gimmicky |

## 4. Writing rules

### Structure
- **Problem first, then Prime.** Open with a recognisable everyday moment (a scratchy throat, a ₹1,200 bill, a laddoo at a wedding) before the product.
- **Two-line headlines** that set up a tension and then resolve it:
  - "Your health insurance covers hospital bills. / Prime covers the rest."
  - "The bills don't start / at the hospital."
  - "Covered in under / 2 minutes."
  - "Every rupee, / disclosed upfront."
- **Benefit card formula:** a short "For…" / "Because…" lead-in that names the human need, then the mechanic.
  - "For the visits you cannot postpone." / "For prescriptions that quietly add up every month." / "Because a test is often the first step to feeling better."
- **Triplets with negation** for reassurance: "No reimbursement. No waiting. No receipts."

### Numbers
- Use **₹ with Indian digit grouping**: ₹3,999, ₹20,000, ₹1,40,000, and **lakh/crore** (₹3L Cr), not millions or billions.
- Lead with the **monthly equivalent** in acquisition copy (₹333/mo) and show the annual price nearby (₹3,999/yr).
- Make time concrete: "2 minutes", "Day 1", "15 days", "7–10 working days".

### Vocabulary

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| everyday healthcare, everyday care | primary care services (fine in the mission, too clinical elsewhere) |
| OPD wallet, cashless, scan & save | claim settlement, reimbursement process |
| doctor visits, medicines, tests | consultations, pharmaceuticals, investigations (in headlines) |
| out-of-pocket | OOPE |
| network partner, partner clinic | empanelled provider (except in legal copy) |
| plan | policy (see §6: Prime Care is not an insurance policy) |
| Get covered → / Find my plan → | Buy now, Submit, Learn more |

### Grammar and mechanics
- **British/Indian spelling:** hospitalisation, paediatrician, programme, centre.
- **Second person** ("you", "your family") for the customer; **"we"** for Prime Care.
- **Sentence case** in body copy. Title Case only in blog H1s and SEO titles.
- **Arrow CTAs:** "Explore Plans →", "Get covered today →", "See my recommendation →".
- **Middot separators** for trust strips: "No waiting period · Cashless benefits · 15-day refund promise".
- Define each acronym on first use: "OPD (outpatient)", "AHC = Annual Health Check-up".
- Use contractions ("don't", "you're", "it's"). They keep the tone conversational.
- Light Indian cultural references are welcome where they're natural (a laddoo at a wedding, the chemist next door, a Netflix comparison). Don't force them.

## 6. Consistency and risk flags

These are places where the current copy works against the "honest" positioning. Fix them first: for a brand called "India's Honest Health Plan", a contradiction between the marketing copy and the T&C is the most damaging tone problem it can have.

| # | Issue | Where | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Insurance language for a non-insurance product.** The T&C call it a "health benefit programme", but copy says "policy delivered", "digital policy card", "OPD cover insurance plan", and "We partner with IRDAI-licensed insurers, so your benefits are real." | Home, About, blog | Have compliance confirm the regulatory status. Default to "plan" and "plan card". Avoid "insurance", "policy" and "premium" when describing Prime itself. |
| 2 | **"Activates immediately" vs "up to 2 working days".** | Home ("Every Prime plan activates immediately"), FAQ ("active the moment your KYC is complete") vs T&C §7 | Pick one truth. If 2 days is possible, say "usually live within minutes, at most 2 working days". |
| 3 | **"No conditions… no fine print"** next to real sub-limits, a ₹1,000/transaction cap, a ₹1,500 AHC deduction and exclusions. | Home, "No waiting period" card | Drop "no fine print". Use "no hidden fine print. Every limit is on our coverage page." |
| 4 | **"Full refund… no questions asked"** applies only if no benefits have been used. | FAQ, About ("refund guarantee"), CTA strip ("promise") | Keep the condition in the same sentence: "Full refund within 15 days if you haven't used any benefits." Settle on one term: "15-day refund promise". |
| 5 | **"Just your name, phone, and email"** for KYC, while the privacy policy collects PAN, date of birth and address. | Home, How it works | Say "Name, phone, email and PAN. No medical tests." |
| 6 | **"Cashless. Everywhere."** The benefit only works at network partners, capped at ₹1,000 per scan. | Home benefit card | Use "Cashless across our partner network." |
| 7 | **Family plan availability.** The home toggle implies a Family option on all tiers; the coverage page shows Essential Family as N/A. | Home vs Coverage | Make the Family toggle hide or disable Essential. |
| 8 | **Name drift:** Prime / Prime Care / Prime.care / prime care. | Site-wide | **Prime Care** in all customer copy. "Prime" alone only in the hero hook. **getprime.care** as the URL. Never "Prime.care" as a byline. |
| 9 | **Dash style:** a spaced hyphen ( - ) is used as a dash. | Site-wide | Use an en dash with spaces ( – ) or an em dash, consistently. |

## 7. Copy patterns to reuse

**Hero:** "[What they already have] covers [the rare thing]. / Prime Care covers the rest."

**Benefit card:** "**[Benefit].** For [the everyday moment it shows up]. [How it works, cashlessly]."

**Reassurance strip:** "Plans from ₹333/month · No waiting period · 15-day refund promise"

**Closing CTA:** "Don't wait for [the bad moment] to [the sensible action]. [Price]. [Speed]. [Risk reversal]."

**Blog intro:** an everyday scene with a rupee figure → "Why? Because…" → the stake for the reader → "Let's actually get into it."

**Blog product tie-in:** one short "Where Prime Care fits" section that quotes the concrete number (e.g. "cashless dental up to ₹2,000 a year") plus a disclaimer that third-party options mentioned are independent.

## 8. WhatsApp advisor messages

These are sent by a Health Advisor after a sales call, one per call outcome. They're the most personal touchpoint the brand has, so they should sound like the website's friend-who-read-the-fine-print, typing on a phone.

### 8.1 Audit of the current templates

What works: every message has a clear trigger, a warm opening, a soft single ask and a named human advisor. "Not Interested" and "Disconnected" already strike the right tone.

| # | Issue | Templates affected | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| W1 | **Off-message: "claims process".** Prime's core promise is that there are *no* claims: you scan the Alyve QR and pay from your wallet. Mentioning a "simple claims process" undercuts the cashless pillar and sounds like generic insurance. | Needs More Info, Comparing Options | Say "how you pay: scan your Alyve QR, no bills to submit". |
| W2 | **Name drift: "Primecare"** (one word) appears in 5 of the 8 templates. "Prime Care Plan" uses a capital P on "plan". | All | Use **Prime Care** (two words), and lower-case "plan". |
| W3 | **No numbers.** The website leads with ₹333/month, no waiting period and the 15-day refund. The templates give none of them, so the customer has to click to find out why Prime Care is different. | Intent Shown, Needs More Info, Comparing, Value-Led | Add a 2–3 bullet recap with the price, no waiting period and the refund condition. |
| W4 | **Generic, interchangeable copy.** "Thanks for your interest… happy to clarify… do have a look" could come from any insurer. The pillars (everyday care, cashless, dental and eye care) are missing. | Needs More Info, Follow Up | Name the everyday benefits: doctor visits, medicines, tests, dental, eye care. |
| W5 | **Formal, email-style sign-off.** "Warm regards" / "Regards" and "Looking forward to assisting you" read like email. | All | Drop the valediction. End on the ask, then the signature block. |
| W6 | **Promise mismatch.** "I've attached the health plan details tailored to your needs" and "I've shared the OPD plan details" are followed by a generic plans link. | Intent Shown, Disconnected | Say what's actually sent: "Here are the plan details." Only say "tailored" if a recommendation is actually attached. |
| W7 | **Comparison copy doesn't use the real differentiators.** No waiting period, dental and vision in every plan, transparent limits, no agent commissions and the refund are all left out. | Comparing Options | Give the customer the questions to ask every provider, with Prime's answers. |
| W8 | **Formatting debris.** There are leading spaces before lines and signatures, and inconsistent " — " spacing. | All | Clean these up before submitting the templates to WhatsApp Business for approval. |
| W9 | **Unsubstantiated social proof.** "Many families choose Primecare", "what our customers value most". | Comparing, Value-Led | Use a concrete everyday cost example instead, or a real stat you can back up. |

### 8.2 WhatsApp writing rules

1. **Open with the customer's name and context** from the call ("thanks for your time today", "looks like our call dropped").
2. **Keep the body to 40–90 words.** That's one screen on a phone, with no scrolling.
3. **Use bullets (•) for facts:** at most 3 bullets, each starting with the benefit.
4. **Include one link per message.** Use `https://getprime.care/#plans`, or `/coverage` when the customer is comparing. Add UTM tags per variation if you're tracking conversion.
5. **Make one ask, and end with it,** as a question the customer can answer in a word ("Call or chat here?").
6. **Use 0–1 emoji in the body.** 📞 is fine in the signature.
7. **Keep conditions in the same sentence:** "Full refund within 15 days if you haven't used any benefits." Never just "no questions asked".
8. **Don't say "claim", "policy", "premium" or "insurance"** about Prime Care itself (see flag #1).
9. **Use this signature block:**
   ```
   {Advisor Name}
   Health Advisor · Prime Care
   📞 {Phone Number}
   ```
10. **WhatsApp Business:** messages sent more than 24 hours after the customer's last reply need Meta-approved templates. Placeholders become `{{1}}`, `{{2}}` and so on. Follow Up and Value-Led Nudge will likely be classed as *Marketing*, so add an opt-out quick-reply ("Stop updates").

### 8.4 Gaps in the sequence

The sheet covers the pre-sale call outcomes only. To keep the voice consistent across the whole customer lifecycle, these stages still need templates:

- **Payment done, activation pending:** set the expectation for the up-to-2-working-days window (see flag #2).
- **Plan active:** the Alyve QR is ready, with how to use it the first time.
- **First-use nudge:** "Book your free Annual Health Check-up" (₹1,500 comes from the diagnostics wallet; say so).
- **Refund window closing:** a day-12 check-in before the 15-day free-look period ends.
- **Wallet balance or mid-year reminder:** unused balances don't carry forward.
- **Renewal:** 30, 15 and 3 days before expiry.
- **Payment link abandoned:** the customer started enrolment but didn't pay.
